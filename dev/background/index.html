Background

This page summarizes the formulation implemented in DDMFaultSlip.jl.

The Displacement Discontinuity Method

A fault or fracture is a surface $Γ$ across which the displacement field of the surrounding elastic medium is discontinuous. The displacement discontinuity (DD) has a normal component, the opening $w$, and a tangential component, the slip $δ$. In an infinite, homogeneous, linear elastic medium, the tractions acting on $Γ$ depend linearly on the DD through boundary integrals.

The Displacement Discontinuity Method discretizes $Γ$ into $N$ elements, each carrying a uniform (piecewise constant) DD. The tractions are evaluated at the element centroids (collocation points), which gives

\[σ_i = σ_i^0 + \sum_{j=1}^{N} E_{ij}\, w_j, \qquad τ_i = τ_i^0 + \sum_{j=1}^{N} E_{ij}\, δ_j,\]

where $σ^0$ and $τ^0$ are the initial normal and shear stresses, and the influence coefficients $E_{ij}$ are the stress at the centroid of element $i$ due to a unit DD on element $j$. Normal stresses are positive in compression. DDMFaultSlip.jl uses closed-form influence coefficients for:

For 3D shear problems with a non-zero Poisson's ratio $ν$, the two slip components are coupled and both are solved for. With $ν = 0$, they decouple and a single component is solved for.

Hierarchical matrices

The elastic matrix $E$ is dense: storing it and multiplying it by a vector costs $O(N^2)$. DDMFaultSlip.jl stores it as a hierarchical matrix using HMatrices.jl. The element centroids are clustered, and blocks of $E$ coupling well-separated clusters (controlled by the admissibility parameter hmat_eta) are compressed to low-rank approximations with adaptive cross approximation (tolerance hmat_atol). Storage and matrix-vector products then scale roughly as $O(N \log N)$, which makes large 3D meshes tractable.

Interface laws

The behaviour of the fault or fracture is described by a local relation between the traction and the DD of each element. Frictional faults are modelled as a thin elasto-plastic interface: the slip increment $Δδ$ is split into an elastic and a plastic (frictional) part, $Δδ = Δδ^e + Δδ^p$, and

\[τ = τ_{old} + k \left(Δδ - Δδ^p\right), \qquad F(τ, σ', Δδ^p) = τ - f\, σ' - η \frac{Δδ^p}{Δt} \le 0,\]

where $k$ is the shear stiffness of the interface, $f$ the friction coefficient, $σ' = σ - p$ the effective normal stress, and $η$ an optional viscosity. The stiffness $k$ is typically taken as $μ / h$ with $h$ a small fault thickness, so that the elastic slip is negligible: it acts as a penalty enforcing stick where the fault does not slide.

For each element, the plastic slip is computed with a return-mapping algorithm: a trial traction $τ^{tr} = τ_{old} + k Δδ$ is computed, and if $F(τ^{tr}) > 0$, the plastic slip increment $Δδ^p$ is found such that $F = 0$. The available laws are:

  • ConstantFriction: constant friction coefficient $f$.
  • SlipWeakeningFriction: friction decreasing linearly with slip from $f_p$ to $f_r$ over the slip $δ_r$.
  • ConstantYield: constant shear strength, independent of $σ'$.
  • DugdaleCohesiveZone: cohesive zone for opening problems.
  • FunctionConstraint: a user-defined stress change as a function of the position and the DD increment.

Fluid coupling

A pore pressure $p(\mathbf{x}, t)$ acting on the fault reduces the effective normal stress, $σ' = σ - p$, and therefore the frictional strength $f σ'$. With FunctionPressure, the pressure is prescribed as a function of space and time, for instance an analytical solution of pore pressure diffusion from an injection point. Pressure increases can then trigger frictional slip, which redistributes the shear stress elastically along the fault.

Nonlinear solver

At each time step, the DD increments $Δu$ (opening or slip) of all elements must satisfy simultaneously the elastic relation and the interface laws:

\[R_i(Δu) = \sum_{j=1}^{N} E_{ij}\, Δu_j - Δt_i(Δu_i) = 0,\]

where $Δt_i$ is the traction increment given by the interface law of element $i$. This nonlinear system is solved with Newton's method. Its Jacobian is the elastic matrix plus a (block) diagonal matrix of local tangent stiffnesses, $J = E + D$, so only $D$ changes between iterations. Each linear system is solved with a Krylov method (IDR(s) by default, GMRES or BiCGStab(l)) from IterativeSolvers.jl, preconditioned with a hierarchical LU factorization of $J$. The factorization is expensive, so it is reused across nonlinear iterations and time steps and only recomputed when the linear solver needs too many iterations or does not reach an accurate solution.

The problems are quasi-static: time enters through the prescribed pore pressure, the viscosity $η$ and the evolution of the interface laws. The time steps are controlled by a time stepper (ConstantDT or TimeSequence), and a time step for which the Newton solver fails is retried with half its size.
